Goals

What you're saving for, and how likely it is

Each goal is replayed in 2,000 simulated markets, good years and bad, after inflation. The odds show how often it gets there.

Which growth rate should I use?

  • Within 3 years — keep it safe: Tagesgeld 2–3%, Indian FD 6–7%.
  • 5+ years — a global equity ETF has averaged ~6–8% a year over long periods, but can drop 30–50% in a bad year.
  • Inflation — ~2% for euro goals, ~5% for rupee goals.

Goal ideas for your situation

  • Emergency fund — 6 months of spending in Tagesgeld.
  • Your child's education — 12–15 years away → equity ETF Sparplan.
  • Retirement top-up — the gap between your expected German pension and the income you want.
  • Flat interiors & possession costs — furniture, maintenance deposit, registration.

    Your monthly order of play

    1. Pay tax deadlines — penalties are the worst “investment”.
    2. Emergency fund to 3–6 months.
    3. Set money aside for known big payments (property stages, school fees, tax bills).
    4. A monthly ETF savings plan for goals 5+ years away.
    5. Only then: single stocks or trading, with money you can afford to lose.

    Allocation

    Rules of thumb

    Spread the risk

    One all-world ETF owns ~4,000 companies. A single stock can go to zero.

    Fees compound too

    A 1.5% yearly fee eats about a quarter of your gains over 20 years. Look for TER under 0.3%.

    Tax on gains

    Most countries tax profits when you sell; check your country's calculators on the Tax tab.

    Funds from another country

    Foreign funds can be taxed differently where you live — check before buying.

    Currency drift

    Returns in a weakening currency look bigger than they are in your spending currency.

    Time, not timing

    Monthly investing beats waiting for the “right moment” for most people.
